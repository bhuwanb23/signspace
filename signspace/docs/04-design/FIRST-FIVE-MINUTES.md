# First Five Minutes

## Beat sheet

| Time | What happens | Why it is here |
|---|---|---|
| **0:00–0:03** | Cold start. The room, uninterrupted. Two hands fade in already mirroring the user | Time-to-first-interaction target <15 s. No logo, no splash, no calibration screen |
| **0:03–0:10** | A reference hand fades in ~40 cm away holding **B**. It rotates **very slightly** on its own — the only affordance in the scene | Motion is the only tutorial that needs no words |
| **0:10–0:18** | The user pinches it. It turns with their wrist. **This is the discovery.** | The product's whole premise in one gesture. Nothing else has happened yet and the idea has already landed |
| **0:18–0:30** | They shape their hand; the match meter leans toward the target. **First lock at ~0:45 in the video, but a real user lands it around 60–75 s** | A first payoff inside 75 seconds; Best First Five Minutes is won or lost here |
| **0:30–0:50** | The B dissolves, becomes an object, arcs to the shelf on their real desk. The chime | The object is *progress made physical*; the shelf is the reason to return |
| **0:50–1:30** | **U** and **V**, back to back. The near-miss fires on V: *"That's U — your fingers are together."* | Minimal-pair teaching, delivered as information, not correction. This is the novelty moment |
| **1:30–2:30** | **W**, then the C↔O pair — the best demonstration in the app | C and O look nearly identical from the front and obviously different in depth. This is the shot that proves the medium |
| **2:30–4:00** | Free practice; the shelf fills; the honest-mirror object becomes visible | Agency. The user is now driving |
| **4:00–4:45** | One gaze inspection: look at a finger, it highlights with its position | The gaze criterion, demonstrated in 45 seconds of organic play |
| **4:45–5:00** | The shelf, five objects, **one empty slot**. A single line: *"Tomorrow: one more."* | The open loop. Three words, and they are the only three in the first five minutes |

## The rules this page encodes

1. **Nothing is explained.** Every instruction is a shape, a motion, or a sound.
2. **Agency before instruction.** The user grabs before they are told to.
3. **The first object appears within 75 seconds** or the design has failed.
4. **The near-miss arrives before the second shape is mastered** — it is the product, not an easter egg.
5. **One empty slot at the end.** Curiosity beats a completion screen.
6. **Text budget: 3 words in five minutes.** Three, and they are "Tomorrow: one more."

## What we will watch for on Day 3

| Failure | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| User reaches for the hand but misses it | Target too small or too far | Enlarge the grab volume; pull the reference 5 cm closer |
| User tries to *push* the hand rather than pinch it | Affordance reads as a button | Make the hand visibly react to proximity before contact |
| User shapes their hand but never notices the meter | Meter out of the gaze cone | Move it into the ~55° cone, beside the reference hand |
| User gets a near-miss and stops | The line reads as an error | Rewrite the copy; soften the sound; never fire twice quickly |
