**What changed in the code:** the file had exactly one behaviour (damped 1:1, hard-coded, never compared
against anything). It now has all three variants behind a `RotationMode` switch, a controller that walks
the six cells in a fixed order, a left-hand pinch gesture to rate 1–5 in the headset, and per-frame
measurement of both the lag and the drift that step 5 of this task asks for.

## Why this exists
**This is the make-or-break test of the whole concept.** If grabbing a floating hand and turning it with your wrist feels wrong, the product's core gesture is wrong and we re-plan on Day 2 rather than Day 8.

## Do this
1. Make the grabbed object a proxy for a hand (a simple hand block or the P1.1 mesh).
2. Attach rotation to wrist quaternion delta, with position held stable near the grab point.
3. Test three variants and rate each 1–5: (a) direct 1:1 wrist coupling, (b) damped 1:1 with slight lag, (c) hold-still-to-rotate with the hand held in place.
4. Try all three at arm's length (~0.4 m) and at lap distance (~0.6 m) — the two distances the product will use.
5. Write a verdict: which variant, and whether the gesture is good enough to build a product on.

## Done when
- [ ] A chosen rotation variant that feels natural at both distances — ⬜ **device**
- [ ] A written go/no-go with a reason — ⬜ **device**; the template and the pre-registered decision rule exist
- [ ] Position drift under rotation measured (should be near zero) — 🟡 **instrumented** every frame; note that drift is **zero by construction** in variants A and B because position is never written during a grab, so the discriminating number is lag, not drift

- Following through to the next task without writing the verdict down
