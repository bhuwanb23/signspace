## Why this exists
FoV-awareness is a named rubric technology, and the VR Glasses profile is 70°×66° against Quest's 110°×96°. A shelf designed for a wide cone simply disappears on the narrow one.

## What was built

`Scripts/Spatial/ShelfReflow.cs` measures the display instead of guessing: the horizontal FoV is
derived from the eye camera's vertical FoV and aspect (or forced by `overrideHorizontalFovDegrees`
in the check), the shelf's distance comes from the head in the shelf's own frame, and the grid's
angular width is `2·atan(halfWidth / distance)`. Below **85°** horizontal FoV it folds:

| profile | columns | grid angular width | widest object edge | inside 55° cone |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 110° (Quest 3S class) | 6 (2 rows) | **51.6°** | 27.5° | at the 27.5° edge — wide display has room |
| 70° (VR Glasses) | 4 (3 rows) | **35.1°** | **22.2°** | yes, with 5.3° to spare |

`ShelfAnchor.SetLayout` does the fold: it carries the objects in world space, rebuilds the slots,
tilts the tray 20°, brings it 4 cm closer and narrows the plate. **No object is scaled** — that is the
trap the task names — and the check asserts each object's world scale is unchanged before and after.
The move is eased over `reflowSeconds` (0.35 s) rather than snapped. The layout is deliberately not
persisted: `layoutVariant` records the grid the save was written against, and the display decides
how it is presented.

The trigger is the measured display, not a fixed 50°: the 51.6° grid is comfortable at 110° and
cramped at 70°, so FoV is the honest variable. The 50° cap is still enforced, as an assertion that
the **tray** is under 50° wide.

`showSafeCone` draws the 55° cone with `Gizmos` for the video and is off in the scene; the draw is
development-only.
