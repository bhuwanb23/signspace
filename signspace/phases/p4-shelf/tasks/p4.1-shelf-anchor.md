# P4.1 — Anchor the shelf to a real surface

**Phase:** P4 · **Est:** 2 h · **Depends on:** P1.6 · **Workstream:** W2
**Gate fed:** G4

## Why this exists
The product's promise is that your room accumulates your progress. That promise is one anchor and one layout rule.

## Do this
1. Query MRUK for horizontal surfaces within a 0.3–1.0 m band and 60° of forward.
2. Score candidates by: proximity to the user, height (waist-level preferred), and free area for a 0.45 m shelf.
3. Place the shelf at the chosen anchor, rotated to face the user, with a 2 cm clearance above the surface.
4. If no surface qualifies, go straight to the plinth path (P4.4) — never block.
5. Log which condition was chosen for every launch (useful for P8 and for the video).

## Done when
- [x] Shelf lands on a real surface and faces the user — `ShelfAnchor` places at the surface point +
      2 cm, rotated to face the user on yaw, parented to the **anchor** so a reset does not move it
- [x] Selection logic logged — one JSONL line per launch (§3.3), written after `Begin()` returns so no
      outcome path can skip it; the finder's rejection counters ride along, so "MRUK found nothing" is
      never the whole story
- [x] No-surface case routes to the plinth without delay — `UsePlinth()` is called directly from the
      failure path; no timeout, no message, no retry loop in front of it

## What was built, and what the simulation found

**The rule** (MRUK-SHELF §3.1): horizontal · label (never FLOOR) · free area ≥ 0.45 × 0.20 m ·
**reach 0.30–1.00 m measured horizontally** · **inline within 60° of forward measured on yaw** ·
height 0.75 ± 0.35 m. Score: height 2.0 · free area 1.0 (capped) · proximity 1.0 · label 1.0/0.4 ·
facing 0.5.

`scripts/shelf_sim.py` mirrors that rule over seven synthetic rooms plus the area-cap property, and
**reads the constants out of the C#** so the mirror cannot drift. It found three real defects in the
shipped finder:

| Defect | Why it mattered |
|---|---|
| reach was the **3D** distance, not horizontal | a waist-high table 0.6 m in front of a standing user is 1.0 m away in 3D (0.8 m of vertical drop), so the most natural shelf in any room failed a limit meant to describe reach |
| the forward cone did not exist; facing was a **soft bonus** | "within 60° of forward" was a spec line with no filter behind it. Worse, the obvious fix — a 3D cone — rejects every waist-high surface for being 40° below the eye line. The cone is measured on **yaw** |
| reach was measured to the anchor **centre**, and the surface point was queried *near the anchor* | the lateral twin of defect 1. A 1.6 × 0.9 m table 1.2 m away has its edge 0.75 m from the user, but the old query returned a point near the middle, so the table was rejected as out of reach — and had it passed, the shelf would have been placed where nobody can reach it. The query now asks the SDK for the surface point nearest **the user**, which puts the shelf on the edge (as §3.2 always claimed) and lets the SDK do the rect math in the anchor's own frame, so a table rotated to face you is handled correctly |

Also added: the area term is **capped** at 0.75 × 0.40 m. Uncapped, a 1.6 × 0.9 m table beat a
0.6 × 0.4 m one by +1.29; capped, by +0.10 — measured in the sim, not asserted in a comment.
