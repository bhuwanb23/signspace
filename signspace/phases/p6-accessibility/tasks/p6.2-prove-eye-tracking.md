# P6.2 — Prove eye tracking is actually active — not silently head-raycasting

**Phase:** P6 · **Est:** 1 h · **Depends on:** P6.1 · **Workstream:** W2
**Gate fed:** G6

## Why this exists
The most common way to fail the gaze criterion is to think you implemented gaze while the rig quietly fell back to head direction. A judge who moves their head and sees the highlight follow will know instantly.

## Do this
1. Log the active input source at runtime (eye vs head) to the Immersive Debugger.
2. Test the discriminator: hold your head still and move only your eyes. The highlight must move.
3. Test the reverse: hold your eyes on one finger and move your head. The highlight must **not** follow the head.
4. Add a visible forced-fallback state and its explanation if eye tracking is unavailable or declined.
5. Record a video of the head-still/eye-move test for the submission.

## Done when
- [ ] Eye-only movement moves the highlight — **NOT RUN** (no eye-tracking hardware; HUMAN-TASKS H15)
- [ ] Head-only movement does not move it — **NOT RUN** (H15)
- [x] Fallback state exists and is honest
